          <h2>Level 1: The Interactive Mystery Case File</h2>
          <p><strong>Difficulty:</strong> 4/10</p>
        </header>

        <section>
          <h3>The Concept</h3>
          <p>
            Build a website that presents a fictional unsolved mystery. You may
            also use a real case; if you do, be factual.
          </p>
          <p>
            Imagine a detective has given you access to a digital case file.
            Your job is to organize all the evidence so another investigator can
            solve the case.
          </p>
          <p>
            Try your best to make it feel like an engaging site, not a plain
            webpage. If you use only HTML, your site may look plain. Don't
            worry; we'll change that with CSS.
          </p>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>Your Mission</h3>
          <p>
            Create or find a case file about an incident. Your site must
            contain:
          </p>
          <ol>
            <li>
              Case overview
              <ul>
                <li>Case number and status</li>
                <li>Incident date and location</li>
                <li>A short incident summary</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Suspect profiles
              <ul>
                <li>
                  Suspects' names, profiles, addresses, occupations, and
                  statements, if any
                </li>
                <li>Their possible motives</li>
                <li>
                  A short summary of where they were at the time of the crime
                </li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Evidence archive
              <ul>
                <li>A timeline of events</li>
                <li>Photographs or illustrations of evidence</li>
                <li>
                  An evidence table containing timestamps, descriptions, and
                  reliability ratings
                </li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Witness statements
              <ul>
                <li>
                  Statements presented using appropriate quotation elements
                </li>
                <li>Contradictions between different witnesses</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Investigator's notes
              <ul>
                <li>Important observations</li>
                <li>Unanswered questions</li>
                <li>A final theory about what might have happened</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
          </ol>
          <p>
            <strong>Completion test:</strong> Can another person understand the
            case analysis without your intervention?
          </p>
        </section>
      </article>

      <article id="level-2">
        <header>
          <h2>Level 2: The University Course Explorer</h2>
          <p><strong>Difficulty:</strong> 5.5/10</p>
        </header>

        <section>
          <h3>The Concept</h3>
          <p>Build a mini university course catalog.</p>
          <p>
            Imagine you're creating a website for a fictional university where
            students can explore a Computer Science degree, understand its
            requirements, and plan their academic journey.
          </p>
          <p>
            This is more than a page listing courses. It's a structured
            information system.
          </p>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>Your Mission</h3>
          <p>
            Create a catalog for a fictional four-year Computer Science degree.
            Your site should include:
          </p>
          <ol>
            <li>
              Degree overview
              <ul>
                <li>Degree name and description</li>
                <li>Admission requirements</li>
                <li>Total credits required</li>
                <li>Graduation requirements</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Course catalog
              <ul>
                <li>Introduction to programming</li>
                <li>Data Structures and Algorithms</li>
                <li>Database Systems</li>
                <li>Operating Systems</li>
                <li>Artificial Intelligence</li>
                <li>Machine Learning</li>
                <li>Web Development</li>
              </ul>
              <p>Each course should include:</p>
              <ul>
                <li>Course code</li>
                <li>Course title</li>
                <li>Credit hours</li>
                <li>Course description</li>
                <li>Prerequisites</li>
                <li>Semester availability</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>Academic roadmap</li>
            <li>Course prerequisites</li>
            <li>Frequently asked questions</li>
          </ol>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>The Challenge</h3>
          <p>Create at least three separate HTML pages:</p>
          <ol>
            <li><code>index.html</code> — Degree overview</li>
            <li><code>course.html</code> — Complete course catalog</li>
            <li>
              <code>requirements.html</code> — Graduation requirements and
              academic policies
            </li>
          </ol>
        </section>
      </article>

      <article id="level-3">
        <header>
          <h2>Level 3: The Science Discovery</h2>
          <p><strong>Difficulty:</strong> 7/10</p>
        </header>

        <section>
          <h3>The Concept</h3>
          <p>Build a digital archive dedicated to scientific discoveries.</p>
          <p>
            This could be a small version of a scientific journal combined with
            a research library.
          </p>
          <p>
            Unlike the mystery project, which focuses on storytelling, this
            project focuses on presenting complicated information accurately and
            making it understandable.
          </p>
          <p>
            You could choose discoveries that interest you personally:
            astronomy, artificial intelligence, physics, chemistry, or biology.
          </p>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>Your Mission</h3>
          <p>
            Create an archive containing at least five scientific discoveries.
          </p>
          <p>Possible subjects include:</p>
          <ul>
            <li>The discovery of gravitational waves</li>
            <li>The first image of a black hole</li>
            <li>The detection of exoplanets</li>
            <li>The discovery of penicillin</li>
            <li>The identification of the cosmic microwave background</li>
          </ul>
          <p>Each discovery must have its own HTML page.</p>
          <p>For each discovery, include:</p>
          <ol type="A">
            <li>
              Discovery overview
              <ul>
                <li>What was discovered?</li>
                <li>When was it discovered?</li>
                <li>Who contributed to the discovery?</li>
                <li>Why does it matter?</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Scientific explanation
              <ul>
                <li>The problem scientists were investigating</li>
                <li>The hypothesis or question</li>
                <li>The methods used</li>
                <li>The results</li>
                <li>The limitations of the findings</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Evidence
              <ul>
                <li>Relevant photographs</li>
                <li>Diagrams or figures</li>
                <li>Data tables</li>
                <li>Captions explaining what the evidence shows</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Research references
              <ul>
                <li>Links to scientific papers</li>
                <li>Links to reputable research institutions</li>
                <li>
                  Publication dates and author information, where available
                </li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>A beginner's explanation</li>
          </ol>
        </section>
      </article>

      <article id="level-4">
        <header>
          <h2>Level 4: The Emergency Response Command Center</h2>
          <p><strong>Difficulty:</strong> 8.5/10</p>
        </header>

        <section>
          <h3>The Concept</h3>
          <p>Build a website for managing a fictional citywide emergency.</p>
          <p>
            Imagine a major flood has affected a city. Emergency responders need
            a centralized website containing incident information, resource
            inventories, evacuation instructions, shelter locations, and
            incident reports.
          </p>
          <p>
            Your task is to build the information interface using only HTML.
          </p>
          <p>
            This is a serious test of your ability to structure a large amount
            of related information.
          </p>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>Your Mission</h3>
          <p>
            Create a command center for a fictional city called Northridge. If
            you use a real city, accuracy is important.
          </p>
          <p>A severe flood has affected four districts.</p>
          <p>Your website must contain the following modules:</p>
          <ol>
            <li>
              Incident dashboard
              <p>Display:</p>
              <ul>
                <li>Incident name and status</li>
                <li>Date and time of the latest update</li>
                <li>A summary of the affected districts</li>
                <li>Current response priorities</li>
              </ul>
              <p>
                Use meaningful headings and appropriately structured data
                tables.
              </p>
            </li>
            <li>
              District reports
              <p>Create separate reports for all four districts.</p>
            </li>
            <li>
              Shelter directory
              <p>
                Create a table of at least eight fictional emergency shelters.
                Include:
              </p>
              <ul>
                <li>Shelter name</li>
                <li>District</li>
                <li>Address</li>
                <li>Capacity</li>
                <li>Current occupancy</li>
                <li>Accessibility information</li>
                <li>Available facilities</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Resource inventory
              <p>Track emergency supplies, including:</p>
              <ul>
                <li>Drinking water</li>
                <li>Food supplies</li>
                <li>First-aid kits</li>
                <li>Blankets</li>
                <li>Resource equipment</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Incident reporting form
              <p>Build a form allowing a fictional responder to submit:</p>
              <ul>
                <li>Their name or responder ID</li>
                <li>Incident location</li>
                <li>Incident category</li>
                <li>Severity</li>
                <li>A written description</li>
                <li>Whether immediate assistance is required</li>
              </ul>
            </li>
            <li>
              Operational timeline
              <p>
                Document the response chronologically using timestamps and
                descriptions.
              </p>
            </li>
            <li>
              Official instructions
              <p>
                Create an emergency information section with evacuation
                instructions, shelter guidance, and contact information for the
                fictional emergency.
              </p>
            </li>
          </ol>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>The Challenge</h3>
          <p>Build the entire website across at least five HTML pages:</p>
          <ol>
            <li><code>index.html</code> — Incident dashboard</li>
            <li><code>districts.html</code> — District reports</li>
            <li><code>shelter.html</code> — Shelter directory</li>
            <li><code>resources.html</code> — Resources inventory</li>
            <li><code>report.html</code> — Incident reporting form</li>
          </ol>
        </section>
      </article>

      <article id="level-5">
        <header>
          <h2>Level 5: The Open Web Knowledge Network</h2>
          <p><strong>Difficulty:</strong> 10/10</p>
          <p>This is the most demanding project of the five.</p>
        </header>

        <section>
          <h3>The Concept</h3>
          <p>
            You're no longer building a single website page. You're building a
            small, interconnected knowledge system.
          </p>
          <p>
            Imagine creating your own miniature Wikipedia combined with a
            documentation website.
          </p>
          <p>
            However, instead of building a random encyclopedia, you will create
            a network of interconnected concepts around one subject.
          </p>
          <p>I recommend choosing the architecture of the internet.</p>
          <p>Why?</p>
          <p>
            Because you can explore DNS, IP addresses, HTTP, HTTPS, browsers,
            servers, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and web hosting. You'll also be
            learning concepts that support your full-stack development journey.
          </p>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>Your Mission</h3>
          <p>
            Build a knowledge network with at least 12 interconnected HTML
            pages.
          </p>

          <section>
            <h4>Part 1: The Central Knowledge Map</h4>
            <p>Create <code>index.html</code>.</p>
            <p>This is your central entry point. It should include:</p>
            <ul>
              <li>An introduction to the internet</li>
              <li>A structured index of major concepts</li>
              <li>Links to every topic in your knowledge network</li>
              <li>A suggested learning sequence</li>
              <li>A glossary of important terms</li>
            </ul>
            <p>Organize the topics into categories. For example:</p>
            <section>
              <h5>Web Foundation</h5>
              <ul>
                <li>How the internet works</li>
                <li>What happens when you visit a website?</li>
                <li>
                  The difference between the internet and the World Wide Web
                </li>
              </ul>
            </section>
            <section>
              <h5>Communication</h5>
              <ul>
                <li>HTTP and HTTPS</li>
                <li>DNS</li>
                <li>IP addresses</li>
                <li>TCP and other networking concepts</li>
              </ul>
            </section>
            <section>
              <h5>Web Development</h5>
              <ul>
                <li>HTML</li>
                <li>CSS</li>
                <li>JavaScript</li>
                <li>Frontend versus backend development</li>
              </ul>
            </section>
            <section>
              <h5>Infrastructure</h5>
              <ul>
                <li>Web servers</li>
                <li>Web hosting</li>
                <li>Databases</li>
                <li>Domain names</li>
              </ul>
            </section>
          </section>

          <section>
            <h4>Part 2: Individual Knowledge Pages</h4>
            <p>Each topic gets its own HTML file. For example:</p>
            <ul>
              <li><code>dns.html</code></li>
              <li><code>http.html</code></li>
              <li><code>html.html</code></li>
              <li><code>css.html</code></li>
              <li><code>javascript.html</code></li>
              <li><code>web-servers.html</code></li>
            </ul>
            <p>Each page should contain:</p>
            <ol>
              <li>A definition</li>
              <li>A beginner-friendly explanation</li>
              <li>A more detailed technical explanation</li>
              <li>Examples</li>
              <li>Common misconceptions</li>
              <li>Links to related topics</li>
              <li>References to reliable documentation</li>
            </ol>
          </section>

          <section>
            <h4>Part 3: Build a Genuine Network</h4>
            <p>This is where the difficulty increases.</p>
            <p>Your pages must connect to one another logically.</p>
            <p>For example:</p>
            <p>
              DNS &rarr; IP addresses &rarr; Web servers &rarr; HTTP &rarr; HTML
            </p>
            <p>
              However, the connections should work in both directions where
              appropriate.
            </p>
            <p>
              If someone is reading your HTTP page and encounters DNS, they
              should be able to open your DNS page. If they start with DNS, they
              should also be able to follow a link to HTTP.
            </p>
            <p>
              Create links between related topics, not just links back to the
              homepage.
            </p>
          </section>

          <section>
            <h4>Part 4: Add an HTML Reference Library</h4>
            <p>
              Create a separate reference page containing at least 30 HTML
              elements.
            </p>
            <p>For each element, document:</p>
            <ul>
              <li>Its name</li>
              <li>Its purpose</li>
              <li>A short code example</li>
              <li>A practical use case</li>
              <li>Whether it's a void element, where applicable</li>
              <li>Relevant attributes, if any</li>
            </ul>
            <p>Organize the references into categories, such as:</p>
            <ul>
              <li>Document structure</li>
              <li>Text and semantics</li>
              <li>Links and media</li>
              <li>Lists</li>
              <li>Tables</li>
              <li>Forms</li>
              <li>Interactive elements</li>
            </ul>
            <p>
              Be accurate: HTML elements have different content models and
              rules. Do not assume that every element can be nested inside every
              other element.
            </p>
          </section>

          <section>
            <h4>Part 5: Create a Self-Assessment Section</h4>
            <p>Create an HTML quiz page using ordinary form controls.</p>
            <p>
              Include at least 15 questions covering your knowledge network.
            </p>
            <p>Questions could ask:</p>
            <ul>
              <li>What is the purpose of DNS?</li>
              <li>What is the difference between HTTP and HTTPS?</li>
              <li>
                What does the <code>&lt;main&gt;</code> element represent?
              </li>
              <li>Why should a form control have an associated label?</li>
              <li>
                What is the difference between a semantic element and a generic
                container?
              </li>
            </ul>
            <p>
              You can create the questions and answer choices using HTML alone.
            </p>
            <p>
              However, automatically calculating a score or displaying feedback
              based on answers requires JavaScript or another processing
              mechanism. For this project, the form can serve as a
              self-assessment worksheet.
            </p>
          </section>
        </section>

        <section>
          <h3>The Challenge</h3>
          <ol>
            <li>Build at least 12 interconnected topic pages.</li>
            <li>Include at least 30 documented HTML elements.</li>
            <li>Create at least 40 meaningful links between pages.</li>
            <li>Use a consistent navigation structure.</li>
            <li>Include a glossary and self-assessment.</li>
            <li>Give every page a clear purpose.</li>
            <li>Avoid copying the same generic content across pages.</li>
            <li>Make every internal link work.</li>
            <li>Use semantic HTML wherever appropriate.</li>
            <li>
              Ensure your content remains understandable with CSS disabled.
            </li>
          </ol>
        </section>
