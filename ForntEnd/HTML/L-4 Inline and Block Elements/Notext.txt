Yes, you can do it the other way around too. **An `<article>` can contain `<section>` elements, and a `<section>` can contain `<article>` elements.** Neither element is restricted to containing only the other.

Think about what the content *means*:

- Use **`<article>`** for a self-contained piece of content that could make sense on its own or be reused elsewhere—for example, a project description, a news story, or a forum post.
- Use **`<section>`** to group related content within a larger page or piece—for example, the “Concept” and “Your Mission” parts of a project.

In your projects page, each project is a distinct, self-contained idea, so an `<article>` for each project makes sense. Its “Concept,” “Mission,” and “Challenge” are parts of that project, so they fit naturally in `<section>` elements.

But suppose you had a page section called “Student Projects,” with several independently meaningful project write-ups. You could put the whole collection in a `<section>` and each project in its own `<article>`:

```html
<section>
  <h2>Student Projects</h2>

  <article>
    <h3>Interactive Mystery Case File</h3>
    <p>A project brief...</p>
  </article>

  <article>
    <h3>University Course Explorer</h3>
    <p>Another project brief...</p>
  </article>
</section>
```

A useful test is: **Could this piece stand on its own?** If yes, `<article>` may fit. **Is it a named group of related content within something larger?** If yes, `<section>` may fit.

They can also be nested in either direction when the content calls for it. Choose based on the structure and meaning, not on a fixed nesting rule. A `<section>` should usually have a heading, and an `<article>` should have a heading that identifies its content.
